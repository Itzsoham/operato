Mise — Terracotta & Olive

Four rules the palette enforces

These hold in all five variants — only the hues change. They are what make it a restaurant operations palette rather than a restaurant brand palette.

Rule 01

Red is never decoration

Danger appears only for destructive actions, validation errors and breached thresholds — never as a button, a logo or a chart series that isn't about failure. Scarcity is what keeps a stock-out warning visible.

Rule 02

Interactive and identity are different colours

--primary carries every button, link and active nav item and must clear 4.5:1 with its foreground. --ready is the loud identity tone — it is a fill, never a text colour on light. --gold-text exists for when that tone has to carry words.

Rule 03

Neutrals are warm, never grey

Every neutral carries a yellow-red bias. Against a cool grey, food photography on the Menu screen reads sickly and the product feels clinical. The warmth costs nothing in contrast and does most of the hospitality work.

Rule 04

Status is ordinal, not categorical

An order state is a position in a sequence, not a label. Colour and a five-tick meter encode it together, so the state survives greyscale, projector glare and colour-blindness — none of which are hypothetical in a kitchen.

Neutrals

Ratios are computed against --paper, the true page background. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text and graphical objects.

Brand & semantic

Order lifecycle — the ordinal system

The signature component. Ready is the only filled chip in the system — food is going cold, and it should win every glance. Note it is never bound to --accent: in the olive and cardamom palettes that would collide with Served-green, and in the red-primary palettes with danger.

Placed → Preparing → Ready → Served → Paid
PlacedQuietest live state — nothing is required of anyone yet.
PreparingGold tint. In the fire. Low saturation; there may be twenty at once.
ReadySolid fill, the loudest thing on screen. Highest urgency on the floor.
ServedSuccess green. Resolved, but money is still outstanding.
PaidDeliberately neutral. Closed rows must recede or a busy night looks like chaos.

Chart ramp

Six categorical series, all mid-luminance so none disappears on warm paper, and every one at or above 3:1 against --paper — the WCAG minimum for graphical objects. The bright identity tone is deliberately excluded: it measures around 2:1 as a bare fill and only survives where a darker 1px border supplies the separation, as on the Ready chip.

Service mode — dark

Not a theme, a second density, used on floor and kitchen tablets. The ground is a warm near-black rather than pure black: pure black against a bright kitchen produces halation, and greasy glass shows every smear on it. The key inversion is that the palette's brightest warm tone becomes the primary interactive colour, because on this ground it out-reads the light-mode primary by a wide margin.

Typography

Plus Jakarta Sans for interface — geometric enough to look like software, with slightly humanist terminals that keep it from feeling like a bank. JetBrains Mono for every currency figure, timer, quantity and the SQL that Ask Operato exposes. Both ship tabular-nums, which is non-negotiable: a money column whose digits shift width is unreadable while scanning. Type is identical in all five palettes.
